# IBERFIT Growth OS 2026

## Purpose

This document converts the market research on global fitness and wellness opportunities into a working strategy for IBERFIT. It is intended to guide product, technology, commercial, partnerships and capital-allocation decisions without turning IBERFIT into a collection of disconnected businesses.

Source research: `Oportunidades de negocio, productos y tendencias de fitness y bienestar para Iberfit en Chile` (11 Sep 2026).

## Strategic thesis

IBERFIT should not compete as another gym, generic workout app or equipment-led boutique. The defendable position is a **precision-fitness platform** built around:

1. measurement and longitudinal assessment;
2. human coaching and professional decision-making;
3. specialized programs by customer need/life stage;
4. measurable progress and continuity;
5. distribution through direct premium consumer, companies and professional referrals.

The common infrastructure is **IRI 2.0 / Fitness Passport**. Products should plug into that system instead of creating separate technology stacks.

## Priority portfolio

### Tier 1 — build now

- **IRI 2.0 / Fitness Passport** — shared data and decision layer.
- **IBERFIT Longevity / Strength for Life 50+** — first premium vertical.
- **Small-group progressive strength** — scalable delivery model and better coach-hour economics.

### Tier 2 — validate next

- **IBERFIT Mujer 40+** — cohort product, not a separate business at first.
- **Corporate Health & Performance** — distribution channel using the same IRI and small-group infrastructure.

### Tier 3 — controlled partnerships

- **Exercise Referral / Sport-Santé** — build a small private referral network with doctors, kinesiology and other relevant professionals.
- **Strength Companion for people already receiving GLP-1 treatment** — exercise and function only; no medication recommendations.

### Tier 4 — community / acquisition

- **Hybrid Performance** — 8–12 week cycles, internal tests, events and rankings. Useful for community and acquisition rather than as the core business.

### Defer until data proves the need

- Recovery Lab with significant CAPEX.
- 24/7 microgym / IBERFIT Pod.
- VR/AR as a core product.
- Building proprietary hardware.

## Product architecture

### 1. Data layer — IRI 2.0

Minimum longitudinal model:

- baseline and reassessments;
- training sessions and load/progression;
- goals and completion;
- subjective recovery/sleep/readiness;
- optional wearable data;
- coach observations and alerts;
- consent and permissions;
- progress comparisons: start → 8/12 weeks → 6 months.

The client should see a small number of understandable signals. The coach can access deeper detail.

### 2. Decision layer

Every meaningful measurement should lead to one of four things:

- continue current plan;
- progress;
- regress/modify;
- investigate/refer.

The IRI must record not just data but **what decision changed because of the data**. This creates IBERFIT intellectual property and avoids becoming a dashboard-only product.

### 3. Human-in-the-loop AI

Use AI first for:

- summarizing evolution;
- identifying adherence decline;
- drafting IRI reports;
- segmenting clients;
- preparing coach messages;
- flagging unusual patterns for review;
- suggesting options that require coach validation.

Do not use AI for autonomous clinical diagnosis or automatic load changes without human confirmation.

### 4. Distribution layer

The same platform should support:

- premium direct-to-consumer clients;
- company-funded/co-funded programs;
- professional referrals;
- community/event acquisition.

## Immediate app implications

The current IBERFIT app should evolve toward these reusable capabilities rather than separate one-off screens:

1. **Longitudinal IRI profile** — baseline, retests, trend history and coach decisions.
2. **Program templates** — Longevity, small-group, Mujer 40+, Hybrid Performance.
3. **Cohort engine** — start/end date, participants, common program, individualized modifications, attendance and retest.
4. **Small-group operations** — capacity, booking, waitlist, coach load, attendance and revenue per coach-hour.
5. **Progress dashboard** — 3–4 headline metrics for client; detailed view for coach.
6. **Consent & permissions center** — wearable, health/risk information, partner sharing and marketing separated.
7. **Referral CRM** — source professional, referral reason/scope, consent, status and feedback allowed by permissions.
8. **Corporate reporting** — aggregate participation/outcomes only; no employer access to individual health/fitness data.
9. **Wearables adapter layer** — bring-your-own-device, not a mandatory hardware strategy.
10. **AI coach assistant** — summaries, adherence risk, report drafts and suggested follow-ups.
11. **Security/device center** — trusted devices, recent access, revoke device, recovery. Authentication reliability is part of product trust, especially as IBERFIT accumulates sensitive longitudinal data.

## First commercial packages to test

### IBERFIT Longevity

Positioning: **strength, autonomy and longevity**, not “fitness for seniors”.

Suggested structure:

- initial IRI;
- 12-week program;
- strength, power, mobility, balance and cardiorespiratory components according to profile;
- reassessment at 8–12 weeks;
- progress report and maintenance plan;
- optional small-group tier and premium 1:1 tier.

### Progressive Strength Groups

Suggested structure:

- 6–10 people;
- individualized starting level inside a shared training block;
- progression by pattern and objective;
- coach feedback captured in the app;
- scheduled reassessment;
- business KPI: revenue per coach-hour adjusted for retention and quality.

### IBERFIT Mujer 40+

Start as one cohort, not a new brand. Strength, recovery, mobility and education within training scope. Clinical/nutritional topics should be handled or reviewed by the appropriate professionals.

### Corporate Sprint

12-week pilot for 20–50 employees:

- onboarding/evaluation;
- small-group sessions;
- simple challenges/content;
- opt-in IRI for participants;
- aggregate company reporting;
- individual data remains private.

## Capital allocation

Initial capital should prioritize the shared platform, protocols, acquisition and compliance rather than physical expansion. A useful decision rule from the research is to allocate roughly **70–80% of the initial budget to common infrastructure, Longevity, small-group, training, compliance and acquisition**, leaving **20–30% for experiments and partnerships**.

Do not treat investment ranges in the research as forecasts. Validate every major line with quotes, pilot utilization and unit economics.

## 90-day operating plan

### Days 0–30

- freeze IRI 2.0 data dictionary and outcome taxonomy;
- define Longevity protocol and inclusion/exclusion/safety rules;
- define small-group operating model and coach capacity;
- create consent/privacy data map;
- interview current/prospective 45–70 clients, women 40–60 and HR decision-makers;
- define pricing hypotheses and unit economics;
- add product analytics events to the app.

### Days 31–60

- ship IRI longitudinal MVP inside the existing app;
- build coach decision log and reassessment flow;
- enable cohort/group objects and attendance;
- prepare Longevity landing/sales flow and first cohort;
- prepare small-group scheduling/capacity;
- create corporate pilot deck and referral partner kit.

### Days 61–90

- enroll first Longevity cohort;
- enroll first progressive-strength groups;
- collect baseline and scheduled retest data;
- track conversion, adherence, coach-hour economics and NPS;
- start 5–10 professional referral conversations;
- begin 5 corporate discovery conversations;
- decide next build based on evidence, not feature enthusiasm.

## Decision dashboard

Pilot thresholds to monitor as hypotheses, not industry guarantees:

- evaluation → paid plan conversion: target ≥35%;
- CAC payback: target ≤3 months;
- Longevity 6-month retention: target ≥70%;
- voluntary monthly churn: target ≤4–5%;
- attendance/adherence: target ≥75%;
- monthly IRI progress review: target ≥60%;
- completed reassessment: target ≥70%;
- small-group occupancy after 3 months: target ≥70%;
- small-group revenue/coach-hour vs 1:1: target ≥1.5×;
- corporate activation: target ≥30%;
- first-cycle corporate renewal: target ≥70%;
- referral → evaluation conversion: target ≥50%;
- NPS: target ≥50;
- serious program-attributable adverse events: 0;
- relevant data/privacy incidents: 0.

## Moats to build deliberately

1. **IRI longitudinal dataset and decision history** — not merely measurements.
2. **IBERFIT protocols** — repeatable assessment, programming and reassessment systems.
3. **Coach quality + software leverage** — technology increases coach effectiveness instead of replacing the coach.
4. **Professional referral network** — trust and distribution that low-cost gyms cannot easily replicate.
5. **Measured outcomes** — demonstrate what improves and for whom.
6. **Privacy/security by design** — essential once physical, performance, wearable and referral data accumulate.
7. **Community and cohorts** — improve retention and reduce CAC through referrals.

## What not to become

- a low-cost gym competitor;
- a generic exercise-content app;
- a hardware reseller;
- a medical provider outside IBERFIT's professional scope;
- a collection of unrelated vertical brands;
- a business that collects more data than it can use responsibly.

## North-star positioning

**IBERFIT = a measurable system for physical health and performance over time.**

Commercially, the promise should be simple:

> We know where you are, what needs to improve, what we are doing about it, and whether you are progressing.

The strategic objective is to move from selling trainer hours to owning a repeatable system of assessment, decisions, coaching and measurable progress that can be delivered 1:1, in groups, through companies and through professional referrals.
